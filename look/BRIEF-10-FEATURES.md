# Gemstone & Fine Jewellery Website: 10 Signature Features

## Instructions to Claude

Upgrade the supplied `index.html`, `atelier.html` and `design.html` into an exceptional, phone-first gemstone and fine jewellery experience. This is a design and implementation brief, not permission to publish, charge payments or invent business facts. Inspect the actual project and dependencies before changing it. Preserve existing working functionality. Propose a phased implementation, then build the agreed phase.

The business sells both loose gemstones and jewellery internationally. Its final name is NOT confirmed. Do not invent a new company history. The existing Ascent identity is provisional.

The objective is desire plus buying confidence: beautiful real products, simple decisions and a credible human service. Avoid a generic beige luxury template, a dashboard aesthetic, ornamental glass panels and animation everywhere. These ten concepts are proposals, not claims of industry-first inventions.

## Shared art direction and engineering rules

- Warm porcelain, charcoal type and restrained champagne accents. Let gemstones supply the colour. Use one expressive display face and one readable body face. Keep body copy at least 16px on phones.
- Main navigation: Gemstones, Jewellery, Bespoke, Our Craft, Contact. Clear language before poetic labels.
- Use actual inventory photographs for purchasable stones. Never substitute AI-generated gemstone imagery or alter a stone's colour to improve saleability.
- Interaction starting points: press feedback around 100ms; selection transitions 160–220ms; larger panel transitions 240–320ms. Tune by testing. No animation should delay an action.
- Animate transform and opacity where possible. Reserve image dimensions. Load the hero poster first; defer nonessential video and 3D. No heavy animation library solely for simple effects.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences. Keyboard and screen-reader operation must work. Provide visible focus, readable contrast, approximately 44px or larger touch targets and no hover-only functionality.
- Target good Core Web Vitals: LCP <=2.5s, INP <=200ms, CLS <=0.1 at the 75th percentile when field data exists. Before launch, test a mid-range Android phone and constrained network; do not present lab results as field proof.
- All demo inventory, reports, prices, people and transactions must be visibly labelled as sample data. Show success only when an action actually succeeds.
- Existing source concerns: missing homepage CSS/JS in the supplied files; autogenerated provenance; simulated upload/payment success; repeated preview labelled as a side view; misleading true-scale claims. Fix these before public release. Do not copy them into the new implementation.

## 1. The Stone-to-Jewel Hero

**Customer moment:** “That beautiful stone could become my piece.”

**Design:** A generous editorial hero shows one real loose stone and the actual finished piece made with it. A discreet two-state control reads `The stone / The finished piece`. Headline: “An extraordinary stone. A piece entirely yours.” Actions: `Explore gemstones` and `Create your piece`.

**Motion:** On deliberate selection, crossfade matched photographs with a subtle position adjustment. A small progress indicator identifies the selected state. Do not pretend a photograph is physically morphing into a manufactured object. If there is real production footage, offer `Watch its making` as a separate play action.

**Smart behaviour:** Remember the selected state for this visit. Link directly to the relevant piece or an explicitly labelled past commission. If sold, offer a similar-piece enquiry, not a misleading buy button.

**Phone:** One image above the headline, no full-screen forced intro, no autoplay audio. Static imagery for reduced motion.

**Requires:** Matched real images, stock status and a real product destination. With no matched example, show two clearly separate products instead.

**Acceptance:** Primary action is visible without waiting for animation; no false implication that two unrelated items are the same stone.

**Effort:** Medium. **Build phase:** 1.

## 2. The Real-Light Viewing Room

**Customer moment:** “Now I understand how this stone actually looks.”

**Design:** A product viewer with `Daylight`, `Indoor light`, `Macro` and `Rotation` tabs. Display carat, dimensions and treatment beside the image, with a plain explanation of each.

**Motion:** Short crossfades between actual captured media. A rotation control scrubs a real image sequence or user-started video. Never fake unseen angles from one photograph.

**Smart behaviour:** Explain lighting conditions and camera limitations. Show `Request another view` with the stone ID and requested lighting already filled in. Unavailable views are honestly marked, not generated.

**Phone:** Swipe through media with an equally usable button alternative. Load only the selected high-resolution asset. Zoom opens on tap, not accidentally during scrolling.

**Requires:** Standardised photography and real rotation footage. Warn that screens and lighting affect apparent colour.

**Acceptance:** Switching tabs never recolours the same image; missing footage cannot be mistaken for real footage.

**Effort:** Medium, plus photography. **Build phase:** 1.

## 3. The Guided Gem Finder

**Customer moment:** “I don't need to be a gem expert to choose well.”

**Design:** A short optional assistant, not a chat bubble: intended use, preferred colour, budget and timeframe. Every question includes `I'm not sure`. Show three relevant stones, not thirty.

**Motion:** One question changes at a time with a restrained slide/fade. Back preserves answers. A compact progress label makes the remaining effort clear.

**Smart behaviour:** Filter and rank real inventory using transparent rules. Explain each match: “Within your budget”, “Your preferred colour”, “Available for a pendant consultation”. Suitability guidance must use jeweller-reviewed rules and distinguish recommendation from manufacturing approval. AI is not required.

**Phone:** Large selectable cards and an always-visible Back/Continue area. Allow browsing without finishing the questionnaire.

**Requires:** Reliable inventory fields, approved suitability rules and explicit currency. Missing treatment or origin information remains unknown.

**Acceptance:** Never recommends unavailable stock as available, exceeds a hard budget silently or creates fictional products. No matches produces helpful alternatives, not invented matches.

**Effort:** Medium. **Build phase:** 2.

## 4. The Three-Stone Comparison Tray

**Customer moment:** “I can finally see why these stones differ.”

**Design:** A quiet bottom tray holds up to three shortlisted stones. Open a comparison with consistent photography and rows for price, dimensions, carat, treatment, reported origin, report and availability.

**Motion:** A small thumbnail transition confirms addition, followed by an accessible text announcement. No bouncing shopping-cart theatrics. Respect reduced motion.

**Smart behaviour:** `Show differences only` removes identical rows. Explain trade-offs in plain language from verified fields. Do not create a universal quality score, investment forecast or lab endorsement. Save locally for anonymous users and explain that it is device-specific.

**Phone:** Select two stones at a time for readable side-by-side comparison; switch the third in. Keep labels visible and avoid a tiny desktop table.

**Requires:** Consistent inventory data and safe local persistence. Revalidate price and availability when reopening.

**Acceptance:** Removal, keyboard operation, stale stock and missing fields all work. A shortlist is never presented as a reservation.

**Effort:** Medium. **Build phase:** 1.

## 5. The Guided Bespoke Studio

**Customer moment:** “I can design something personal without making technical mistakes.”

**Design:** Replace the seven-stage wall of options with four clear stages: Piece, Stone, Style & Metal, Review with a Specialist. Desktop has a large preview beside one active decision. Phone has a compact collapsible preview and sticky Continue button. Advanced choices stay inside an optional drawer.

**Motion:** Selection highlights update instantly. Preview changes crossfade without flashing or moving the controls. Completed stages collapse into editable summaries.

**Smart behaviour:** Preserve valid choices when earlier selections change. Explain incompatible choices and ask before removing them. Allow `Help me choose`. Label illustrative renders and price estimates explicitly. An engineer or jeweller approves manufacturability, not an AI model.

**Requires:** Reviewed compatibility rules, real pricing inputs, preview assets and a genuine enquiry endpoint. Use a quotation request until live pricing is dependable.

**Acceptance:** No dead ends; Back restores state; no hidden added charges; errors preserve the design. User-entered text is safely rendered. Do not claim true physical scale on an uncalibrated screen.

**Effort:** Large. **Build phase:** 2.

## 6. The Gemstone Passport

**Customer moment:** “I know exactly what is known about this stone, and what isn't.”

**Design:** `View stone passport` opens a clean document-style panel with the individual stone ID, actual media, measurements, disclosed treatment, available lab report and documented provenance. Use status labels such as `Lab report`, `Seller disclosure` and `Not documented`.

**Motion:** A fast drawer reveal, not a theatrical certificate animation. Clicking a report opens the genuine document or verified issuer destination.

**Smart behaviour:** Separate lab findings from seller statements. Explain what a report does and does not establish. Remove the current randomly assigned cutter names and districts. Never imply an in-house record is an independent lab report.

**Phone:** Short overview first, expandable technical detail, legible report download.

**Requires:** Actual source documents, permission to publish them and carefully maintained product records. Redact personal information where necessary.

**Acceptance:** Every provenance field has a source or reads unknown. No invented report number, origin, ethical claim or authenticity badge.

**Effort:** Medium. **Build phase:** 1.

## 7. The Private Viewing Appointment

**Customer moment:** “A real specialist will show me these exact stones.”

**Design:** A restrained `View these with a specialist` button appears beside the shortlist and passport. A short booking sheet carries the selected stone IDs, interests and preferred language forward.

**Motion:** Smooth expansion into a booking panel; immediate loading feedback and clear success/error states. No intrusive popup on arrival.

**Smart behaviour:** Show visitor-local and Colombo appointment times together. Offer confirmed slots only if connected to a real calendar. Otherwise call it `Request a time` and state that confirmation will follow. Send the specialist a structured brief so the customer need not repeat everything.

**Phone:** Few fields, timezone visible, email confirmation and optional calendar download after confirmation.

**Requires:** Real staff availability, booking/request storage, notification delivery and a privacy notice. Do not expose internal calendar details.

**Acceptance:** No double booking; no false confirmation; selected stones and timezone survive submission. Explain that an appointment does not reserve inventory.

**Effort:** Medium. **Build phase:** 1 for requests; 2 for live scheduling.

## 8. The Private Design Room

**Customer moment:** “My partner and I can choose together without losing our decisions.”

**Design:** A saved collection combines stones, jewellery inspiration and a proposed design. Customers can add notes such as “Love this colour” and “Prefer this setting”. Optional private sharing lets a partner review the same selection.

**Motion:** Gentle card reordering and clear saved-state feedback. Never display `Saved` before persistence succeeds.

**Smart behaviour:** Save configurations by version. Offer an explicitly opt-in `Discreet mode` that removes revealing product descriptions from notification subjects. Explain that this cannot hide browsing history or shared-device activity. No public links by default.

**Phone:** Simple cards with Notes and Compare actions, not a sprawling mood board.

**Requires:** Secure storage for cross-device use. Shared links need high-entropy tokens, expiry, revocation and access checks. Do not put private notes into analytics or public previews.

**Acceptance:** Revoked links stop working; another customer cannot access the room; expired prices are flagged. Local-only saves clearly say they remain on this browser.

**Effort:** Large with secure sharing; small for local shortlist notes. **Build phase:** 2, sharing in 3.

## 9. The Craft Story with Real Evidence

**Customer moment:** “I can see the skill behind what I'm buying.”

**Design:** An editorial sequence shows selection, cutting, design, setting and finishing through actual workshop photographs. Introduce real craftspeople only with permission. Keep captions specific and short.

**Motion:** Gentle once-only reveals; an optional before/after image slider for a genuinely matched process example. Normal scrolling must remain normal. Do not use a full-page pinned animation or forced cinematic loading screen.

**Smart behaviour:** Let visitors follow `The stone` or `The jewellery` to see the relevant process. Link finished examples to available pieces or clearly labelled past commissions. Distinguish a general workshop story from the documented history of a specific item.

**Phone:** Stacked photography, tap-to-play clips with captions, static equivalent for reduced motion.

**Requires:** Original workshop media, permissions and verified captions. No stock imagery presented as the company's own workshop.

**Acceptance:** Story remains understandable without motion or audio; no false heritage, staff counts or production-location claims.

**Effort:** Medium, plus content production. **Build phase:** 2.

## 10. The Commission Journey

**Customer moment:** “I never have to wonder what is happening to my piece.”

**Design:** After a real commission, show a private timeline: Brief confirmed, Design ready, Customer approval, Making, Quality check, Dispatch. Each stage has its actual update, relevant photo and next action. Make `Your approval needed` unmistakable.

**Motion:** Animate a stage change only after a confirmed backend update. Highlight new photographs subtly, not with confetti or fictitious progress.

**Smart behaviour:** Store the exact design version approved by the customer, timestamp and approval terms. Changing an approved design requires a fresh approval. Consolidate notifications. Clearly separate estimated dates from confirmed events. Staff update milestones manually in the first version.

**Phone:** One next-action card first, timeline beneath. Photo uploads can retry without losing comments.

**Requires:** Authenticated customer access, per-order permissions, staff workflow, protected files, audit history and real payment integration if payments are displayed.

**Acceptance:** A customer cannot read another order. Approval cannot silently apply to a revised design. No front-end button can mark a payment as received. Delay updates are honest and preserve history.

**Effort:** Large. **Build phase:** 3, after the sales journey works.

## Recommended build sequence

**Phase 1: Win trust and enquiries.** Features 1, 2, 4, 6 and the request-based version of 7. Use genuine inventory, reliable media and real enquiries. Fix the missing dependencies and misleading prototype behaviours first.

**Phase 2: Improve selection and bespoke conversion.** Features 3, 5, 9 and the basic saved-design part of 8. Test with people unfamiliar with gemstones before expanding the configurator.

**Phase 3: Support the relationship.** Secure sharing in 8 and the customer portal in 10. Do not build these before assigning staff responsibility for keeping records current.

## What to show the business owner in a short demo

Open the Stone-to-Jewel hero. Find a stone by colour and budget. Switch its real lighting views. Compare it against another. Open its sourced passport. Carry it into a simple jewellery design. Request a private viewing with the selections already attached.

This tells one coherent story: **desire → understanding → confidence → personalisation → human service**. It is stronger than ten disconnected visual tricks.

## What NOT to add

- Heavy 3D merely to appear premium. Consider it later only with accurate assets and a useful customer decision to support.
- AI-generated certificates, mining stories, gemstone imagery for actual stock or investment-return promises.
- Fake scarcity counters, fictional reservations or fake recent-purchase popups.
- A chatbot as the default shopping journey. Solve predictable decisions with clear controls first.
- Automatic colour correction that changes the apparent product, autoplay sound or animations blocking purchase.

## Final direction

The website should feel like a private viewing with a knowledgeable jeweller: visually memorable, reassuring and easy to use. The highest-value wow is seeing the actual stone clearly, understanding it and confidently imagining it as your own jewellery.
